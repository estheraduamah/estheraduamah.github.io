Geospatial Redlining investigates how 1930s Home Owners' Loan Corporation (HOLC) neighborhood appraisal grades persist as measurable spatial determinants of urban heat, green-space scarcity, air pollution, and healthcare facility access today.

Using geospatial polygon overlays and census-tract public health indicators, this project quantifies how historical redlining boundaries continue to shape environmental exposure and chronic disease disparities nearly a century after discriminatory lending maps were drawn.

The resulting maps and statistical comparisons translate complex spatial joins into clear, place-based evidence for equitable urban health policy.

01 /

Spatial Joins Across Historical HOLC & Census Geometries

Because 1930s HOLC polygons do not align neatly with modern census tracts, we implemented area-weighted spatial interpolation to align historical grade classifications (A through D) with contemporary environmental and health datasets.

02 /

Quantifying Cumulative Environmental & Clinical Burdens

Across formerly D-graded tracts, overlays revealed compounding disparities in tree canopy coverage, particulate exposure, and transit distance to primary care—demonstrating how historical redlining boundaries continue to shape environmental exposure.
