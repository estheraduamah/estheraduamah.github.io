Justice Chamber is an interactive museum exhibit designed to help visitors explore competing theories of justice by conversing directly with AI personas—each programmed to reason from a distinct moral framework.

By placing visitors face-to-face with conversational agents debating high-stakes ethical dilemmas, the installation invites reflection on how philosophical assumptions shape moral judgment—and how uncanny, persuasive, or unsettling AI can feel when asked to weigh human values.

01 /

My Role & Contributions

Serving as both UI/UX Designer and Project Manager across a six-person team, I guided the exhibit from early conceptual framing through physical fabrication and evaluation:

  • Framing Guiding Questions: Helped define the core research and visitor reflection questions anchoring how visitors compare competing moral philosophies.
  • Medium-Fidelity Prototyping: Designed and built the interactive medium-fidelity prototype in Figma, establishing visual hierarchy, dialogue pacing, and model transitions.
  • Evaluation & Iteration: Planned and conducted heuristic evaluations alongside usability testing to uncover friction points in conversational turn-taking.
  • Hardware & Enclosure Fabrication: Engineered the physical button-sensor inputs and collaborated on designing and laser-cutting the custom exhibit housing.
02 /

How the Prototype Grew

  1. Low-Fidelity Exploration: Explored diverse interaction formats through rapid sketches of visitor actions, early interface screens, and physical kiosk layouts.
  2. Medium-Fidelity Prototype (Figma): Built interactive screens featuring custom chatbot sprites, sample moral dilemma dialogues, and smooth transitions between justice models, validated through usability tests.
  3. High-Fidelity Prototype (HTML & CSS): Developed a functional front-end simulating synchronized digital and physical interactions, iterating on findings from heuristic evaluations and user testing.
  4. Final Deliverables & Curtain Call: Produced a pitch video, final presentation, documentation website, and final report. Upgraded with voice and tactile button controls, Justice Chamber now lives on display in the Olin College Library.
