As an Undergraduate Research Assistant (REU) in the Daub Lab at Karolinska Institutet in Stockholm, I conducted literature reviews on breast cancer biology and developed machine learning approaches linking clinical imaging with molecular tissue architecture.

Our study integrates clinical radiomics and spatial transcriptomics to characterize mammographic density patterns across Estrogen Receptor Positive (ER+) and Triple-Negative breast cancer subtypes.

Findings from this work are being presented at the 2026 Biomedical Engineering Society (BMES) Annual Meeting in Orlando, Florida.

01 /

Cross-Scale Biomarker Alignment

Women in the highest mammographic density category face a four- to six-fold increased risk of developing breast cancer and experience higher rates of interval cancers masked by dense fibroglandular tissue. While elevated breast density has historically been associated with estrogen-responsive biology, how density-related gene programs behave in aggressive, hormone-independent triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) remains poorly understood. We investigated whether clinical and MRI-derived radiomic features can distinguish TNBC from estrogen receptor-positive (ER+) tumors, and how density-associated gene signatures are organized across cell lineages and tissue compartments.

02 /

Subtype Feature Modeling (ER+ vs. Triple-Negative)

We structured quantitative radiomic texture features alongside spatially resolved transcriptomic profiles to compare stromal and epithelial signatures differentiating ER+ and triple-negative breast cancer cohorts.
