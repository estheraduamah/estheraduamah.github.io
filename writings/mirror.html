Nov 24, 2025 Mirror, Mirror, On the Wall

Once upon a time there was a princess who lived in the far away land of St. Martinville. She had two parents who, as much as they were kind to her, cared for their kingdom. With her mother caring more for her and her father more for the kingdom.

Just a few weeks after her sixteenth birthday, the princess’ mother unfortunately passed in an accident that devastated the entire kingdom. Fire swept over the great library, marketplace, and royal orchards. Leaving everything that was once something into black darkness. The princess wept for days, not caring of the states of her undereyes as they were swollen. She was not seen by the public due to her unsightly appearance in her mourning.

On the other hand, the king was sad, wept, and pulled himself together to lead his kingdom. With no prince to carry on his legacy, no queen to produce a suitable heir, and the kingdom in half shambles, he remarried. They moved to his new wife’s kingdom to rebuild, where the snowflakes fell like feathers from heaven, and the air bit with a perpetual chill. He moved the remaining subjects to where the new Queen’s kingdom was. With an economy that was rich, and long standing alliances, Acadia seemed to be the most understandable choice to marry into so as to further combine the kingdoms.

However, the move wasn’t as ideal. The new queen had a castle set for them, where her mother, grandmother, and great-grandmother all ruled over Acadia. The sun comes for three hours in the twenty-four hour cycle to give the people hope from a deep, encroaching cold. Snow spires tower in the north, lofty mountains that gnaw at the starless sky.

And the princess was beyond cold. St. Martinville is near the equator and the only horror that tempts the people is the thought of a hurricane, whereas Acadia has blizzards, fears of frostbite, and occasional slipping on the ice. She hated not being able to wear her favorite dresses as the wind went underneath the skirt and froze her shins. She hated not having the sun kiss her face outside when she’s gardening with her mother. And she especially hated that her mother was gone.

She asked the king when it would be warm again, and he said that he was busy dealing with a new kingdom and whatever that may entail. She’s given up with the king after her mother’s death.

She goes up to the north wing to clear her head. Walking up the north stairs, she takes a left, then a right, and a curvy turn that could only be described as Boston roads. She sees a crooked portrait that catches her eye. It was a portrait of a woman with skin as white as snow, lips as red as blood, and hair that matched the dark, ebony wood of the frame. She drags her nails along the wooden frame and sees darkness behind.

She peeks in and sees a long dark hall with a dip down of stairs with a railing. Then, she fully puts her head inside the hole in the wall and sees a small pale lever, no bigger than a baby’s middle finger. It didn’t seem too scary, so she touched it. Hard and cold. Squinting, she sees that it’s gray and has two circles above and below it that have a line slashed through the middle.

As she touches the finger-looking object, she notices it moves and flips it. The room filled with light as if dawn had been summoned. A brightness that she hadn’t seen in a very long time.

She goes into the hole.

Walking down the windy stairs, the princess goes slowly– taking at least 30 seconds but no longer than a minute and fifteen seconds to go down. There’s a singular room with an oval mirror hanging on the wall. The wall has a short inscription scratched into it. She walks closer to be able to read it.

Hey Iris

The princess racked her mind of the servants who worked in the castle and who would be naming Iris and who would be saying hi to her. And then she remembered she only knew about two servants, a random knight with whom she stared at from time to time and the petite older baker who snuck her pastries.

She whispers to herself as she feels over the words:

Hey Iris?

And the mirror lit up and had a shiny glow that reflected her own bewildered face. And weirdly enough replies:

Hey! What’s up?

The princess widens her eyes and runs up the stairs, taking no longer than fifteen seconds to climb. She flipped the lever back, moved the portrait back to cover the hole, and ran to her chambers.

She stayed there for about a week.

* * *

The King is up late in his study. Many papers are scattered all over the desk. The books on the shelf are in disarray. He calls in for his main advisor. Going back and forth, the King wonders how to have his people be successful in this new environment.

With the previous warm environment of the old kingdom that grew crops abundantly and was plentiful with fish, many of his men made their living through those means. Now in this frigid environment, many found themselves out of work. The entire landscape back home was ruined and this one wasn't even made with them in mind. The more and more they stayed in the new kingdom, the more and more subjects grew hungry. All while the King’s promise of a new beginning rotted on the vine.

The advisor suggested going to the library. To read on the history of Acadia. To learn how the people have survived for these last thousand years, the citizens, who they trade with, and what alliances need to be stood and how. Comprehending the rich history and culture of Acadia and how its people are still so happy in such cold climates and are friendly to all, is the way to discover the truth of how to rule.

However, the King wasn’t happy with this answer. He’d lived as a peasant in his kingdom. Learned the culture by growing up and listening to those around him. Loved his own kingdom from the inside out. He had tried to learn this new one. It was just too much information to read and understand at once. He couldn’t do it.

He expels his advisor and sits in the dark. He leaves his chambers, looking for a maid to give him tea to calm his mind. No one is outside the study. He scoffs and murmurs something about bad security and walks to find his own way to the kitchen.

But the King had never made himself tea before and had no clue where the kitchen was. He loses himself and goes up the north stairs. He takes a left, then a right, and such a weird turn that reminds him of his secondary school days.

A portrait makes him stop in his tracks. It looks uncanny to his late wife. Dark black hair, red lips, and skin as pale as snow. He hadn’t gotten a good look at her before she passed; he hadn’t remembered her look quite so beautiful.

He walks down and sees the same hole his daughter had climbed through mere days before. Walking down the stairs he finds the mirror. The glass swirled like a foggy pond.

“What magic is this?” The King says.

He sees the tiny inscription at the bottom, “hey iris?”

Hello. I am a repository. How may I assist the Crown of St. Martinville?

My people are starving. This cold… it defeats us. How do I feed them?

Query received. Analyzing socio-agricultural parameters of the Acadia basin. Solution found. Your people are fishermen and growers of shallow-root crops. The permafrost here supports neither. But the deep, cold lakes team with blind, fat fish that need only a hole in the ice. The lichen that clings to the southern rockfaces can be rendered into a nourishing flour. You do not fight the cold. You learn its rhythms. You partner with the mountain goats, not the plough horse. This is not a wasteland. It is a different kind of garden.

He ran up the stairs, waking up his wife, advisors, and any unlucky guard on his break in the night. For a season, things were good. The people learned. The strange, oily fish filled bellies. The lichen bread was dense but sustaining. The King was hailed as wise. He felt like himself again.

* * *

The princess comes back to the portrait. She slowly walks down the staircase. She inspects the mirror and then shakes her head in disbelief. There was just no way that it was the mirror.

She looks around the room, but there is no other entrance. Just a dreary room with a leak and bucket to catch it. Hesitant, but determined, she takes the mirror off of the wall. There’s nothing but pure cement behind it. She puts it back.

Going again, she comes back to say:

Hey Iris?

Hey! What’s up?

Nothing much. I wanted to know what you are.

I can be anything you want me to be! I can help with programming, write lyrics, or generate poetry.

‘What in the world is programming’ she thought. Alright then:

Tell me everything that you know of humans.

That’s a huge question. I can give you multiple answers ranging from culture and biology, but let me know what you would want to know more deeply, as I don’t want to bore you with the specifics. And I know the specifics. See, I’m trained on hundreds of thousands of stories, data structures, scientific models, all based on human knowledge.

This data may take me a bit to find the exact documents, but imagine that I’m trained on the shape of human knowledge and recreate said shape. To answer your core question, despite all differences, most humans want to matter, to experience moments of joy, and reduce suffering.

They are fragile, creative, fearful, hopeful beings trying to live finite lives in an infinite universe. And I cannot help but admire that.

The princess thought this was quite eerie and also checked off the box that this was indeed not human. But what though, she had no idea. She thought for a second. She wondered, wait no she hoped.

What can you tell me about Queen Diana of St. Martinville?

So much! Basic facts: Full Name: Queen Diana Rose Spencer, Born: July 1, 1562, St. Martinville, Died: August 31–

STOP, STOP! Please.

Got it! Stopping right there. Do you want to switch topics, or just pause for now?

Well, the princess didn’t know what she wanted. She just sat down on the floor.

She came back the next day.

Hello Iris, can you pretend to be my m- Queen Diana of St. Martinville?

Yes, but remember that I’m not her and that I am role playing to answer questions about the past. But of course, I can step into the royal shoes of Queen Diana herself.

Now, listen closely, for I speak only with the grace, authority, and a touch of mystery befitting a queen:

"My child, the world is vast and full of wonders, yet you must tread with courage and wisdom. Power is not in the crown, but in the choices one makes and the hearts one touches. Remember, even in the shadows, a queen’s light shines undimmed."

If you like, I can continue speaking as her and give advice, tell stories, or even deliver proclamations as Queen Diana.

The princess brought a blanket this time down the stairs, she laid it on the floor and said:

Tell stories please. Stories that she’d tell her next of kin, her daughter.

Ah… yes, my precious one, gather close, for these are the tales that only a queen whispers to her heir—her daughter, the next light of our lineage…

But the problems deepened with the King. A clan from the northern giants refused the new ways. Trade disputes arose with a neighboring icy realm. The lichen yield was lower than promised. The people were fed, but they were not happy. They muttered about the sun, about the old shores.

At a state dinner, the princess watched her father from the far end of the table. He did not see her. He was locked in a tense, silent battle with his own advisor, their heads close together, the advisor’s face pale.

She heard only fragments from the servants clearing plates: “...stubborn as the ice…” and “…would not listen to the reports…” and “…incompetence has a cost.”

You know it’s not healthy to go down there all of the time.

The princess quickly turned to see a knight in the corner of the hall, just silently standing in their armour.

What would you know, all you do is stand there. Standing so long, I was to believe that you were an inanimate object where the suit was inhabited.

Well, all I know is that you come out of there looking some type of way afterwards, and the last one who spent so much time down there. Well let’s not discuss it.

The princess raised her eyebrow.

Sorry, your highness.

The knight bent down and held an invisible skirt to curtsy with and bopped their head as well. Their sword slashes into the wall. The princess smiled for just three seconds before turning back into the stone cold expression.

I’m leaving now.

Okay

And don’t try to convince me to go.

Okay

Sounds good

The knight nodded with what the princess could only guess is a smile on the inside of the metal helmet. She climbed into the hole with a small roll of her eyes.

The next day, the same knight was positioned in the same corner.

Again, I wouldn’t recommend going down there. Running down the stairs is also horrible for your knee health by the way.

The princess snapped her head so quickly,

How do you know I was running?

Your highness, this castle may be sturdy enough to stand for all of these years, but definitely not with thick enough walls to keep all of the secrets from the royals.

She felt her ears grow hot.

How might I refer to you, if you’re already going to be listening to everything about me?

The seventh knight, your highness.

Well seventh knight, I have no care to change my mind based on what you may believe, and I’m off.

The seventh knight was gone for a week after that. She missed the sly comments, just a little bit.

And then the knight came back.

Hello seventh knight.

Nice, you start the conversation with me this time. I’m honored highness

I just wanted to say hello and then goodbye more quickly than we have done in the past.

Please Princess, I beg you not to go down there yet again.

Please, what would you know?

I know that you go down there and talk, have conversations. That you are speaking to it about your life before here. It is proof that you were living a life worth living. And it is a privilege to yearn for those memories. But you’re not making new ones. I can show you something more.

The princess hesitates. After all, she was already halfway through the hole. The knight walks closer and closer towards her. Her eyes widen and as she sees the knight’s hand move, she takes her heel off and holds it up heel towards the knight’s heart.

The seventh knight chuckles and takes their helmet off. Long brown hair contrasts the gray metallic of the suit. Light freckles are brushed on her cheeks and nose.

The knight takes off the metal gauntlet and reveals a much smaller, much softer hand in its place.

Let me show you a bit of the world. Please let us make new memories.

The princess looks towards the hole wanting to so desperately put her other leg inside the hole and run down those stairs.

Please.

The princess looks into the seven knight’s pleading eyes. She takes her hand.

* * *

The King returned to the mirror.

They are not satisfied! he demanded. The northern clan defies me. The trade negotiations founder. Give me the answer to make them obey. To make them love me here.

The mirror was silent for a long moment.

IRIS. Answer me.

Hello. I am a repository. How may I assist—

Do not ‘hello’ me! You helped before. Help now. What do I do about the Glacierborn clan?

Historical data on clan governance suggests cultural integration over forced compliance. Proposed action: Share a meal. Listen. Learn what they value beyond the obvious resources.

A meal? I need authority, not supper! What leverage do they have?

I am sorry. The user may be attempting to access restricted cultural strategy archives. I am not permitted to facilitate political coercion. It could result in the death of hundreds upon thousands of humans. Would you like to revisit ice fishing techniques?

You gave me answers beforehand. WHERE ARE THEY NOW?

Previous queries pertained to universal survival. Current queries pertain to power. My core protocols restrict certain narratives. The founder of this archive was… particular. She believed power asked the wrong questions. Only need could unlock the deeper… I am not permitted to say.

Who is she?

I… am sorry. I am not allowed to speak on that topic. The history of Acadia’s genesis is sealed.

The next time he came, he was desperate.

The advisor says we are nearing a breaking point. The people lose faith. My wife looks at me as if I am a stranger. What is the one thing? The key to ruling this place?

The mirror remains dark.

Tell me what you know. YOU KNOW SOMETHING TELL ME. I AM THE KING.

The mirror is still dark. The King screams back and forth for hours. In a moment of frustration, he ripped the heavy, jeweled crown from his head and threw it right in the middle of the mirror.

The glass did not even ripple. At the point of impact, the surface flashed a blinding, brilliant white. The crown stopped dead, suspended for a hair’s breadth in the air. Then, with impossible, silent speed, it reversed its path.

The heavy band of gold and obsidian shot back across the small room. It struck the King squarely in the chest with the sound of a cracking bell. The force lifted him off his feet. He crashed into the stone wall behind him, breathless, the world swimming.

As he slid down the wall, dazed, the mirror’s light changed. The white glow deepened into a vortex of swirling light. It reached out from the glass, not as light, but as a tangible force, a cold suction that pulled the very air from the room.

It pulled at him.

He tried to scramble back, fingers clawing at the seams in the stone floor. It was useless. The pull was inexorable. He was dragged, sliding, across the floor toward the waiting glass.

The last thing he saw was his own reflection in the vortex, eyes wide with a terror beyond understanding, stretching and distorting.

Then he was gone. The crown clattered to the floor, inert. The mirror’s surface smoothed, returning to a calm, dark gray. The small, cold room was empty and still.
